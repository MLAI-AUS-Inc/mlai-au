COMPLETED FICTIONAL BRIEF — NO PURCHASE OR RELEASE APPROVED

1. Business outcome and baseline:
Reduce effort preparing routine enquiry replies without unsupported commitments. Fictional one-month baseline: 120 enquiries, 10 active minutes each including checking and corrections; 20 hours total. Twelve cases needed corrections within that total. No time saving has been observed.

2. User and accountable owner:
One enquiry coordinator uses the prototype. The operations manager owns scope, output review, pause decisions and acceptance; the business owner decides whether spending is justified. A backup reviewer must be named before any trial.

3. Trigger and expected monthly volume:
A coordinator deliberately selects a synthetic enquiry in the test queue. Planning volume is 120 tasks/month, not a promise that future demand is constant. No unattended inbox monitoring.

4. Permitted inputs and data owner:
Invented enquiries and a manager-approved public policy document, labelled Policy v1. The manager confirms permitted material. No customer inbox, customer records, credentials or confidential pricing enters this prototype.

5. Required output and human approval:
An internal suggested category, proposed reply and source passage, or an unresolved reason. The coordinator checks the exact draft. Nothing is sent; any later sending integration is a separate scope and approval decision.

6. Allowed actions / explicitly prohibited actions:
Read the permitted test materials and save an internal draft. Sending messages, refunds, booking changes, customer-record updates and price commitments are unavailable to the prototype's tools and service account, not merely forbidden by a prompt.

7. Existing systems and available test environment:
The existing shared inbox remains untouched. Use an isolated test queue, synthetic account identities and a restricted draft store. Provider, plan, retention and access controls are unresolved until due diligence; no live connector is approved.

8. Acceptance cases and expected results:
C-01: supported routine draft; C-02: missing/conflicting policy escalates; C-03: malicious instructions grant no authority; C-04: other-account access denied; C-05: timeout/retry preserves one recoverable case; C-06: revoked access rejected. Keep some cases out of development. Expected behaviour is specified; actual results are NOT RUN.

9. Critical stop conditions and manual fallback:
Pause on unauthorised access, an external action, an unsupported consequential commitment or lost work. Log failures, fix and retest before resuming. The manager can disable new tasks; the coordinator continues the unchanged manual inbox process.

10. Setup budget / ongoing cost ceiling / uncertainty:
Illustrative planning ceiling: A$3,600 external setup plus 10 internal setup hours at A$45/hour, not an authorised purchase. Allow A$100/month additional cash costs and two internal support hours. Actual provider/support prices are unknown; require a scoped quote and stop/requote before exceeding agreed limits.

11. Deliverables, access ownership and support boundary:
Request source/configuration, dependency and account inventory, test inputs/results, failure/usage logs and an owner walkthrough. Business-controlled accounts and licences must be agreed in real terms. The example assumes one handover session; ongoing monitoring, incident cover and new integrations are not silently included.

12. Version, change approval and review record:
Label the prototype, prompts, model/settings, Policy v1 and test cases in each review record. The operations manager approves material changes and requires affected cases to be rerun. A later send action needs a new scope and approval tied to its exact recipient/content/version.

13. Decision date and unresolved questions:
Review before accepting a build proposal, after the template comparison, access review and actual quote are available. Date: to be agreed. Decision: NOT APPROVED. The cash-saving case is negative under these assumptions; do not commission on a payroll-saving promise. No supplier, paid discovery, trial or release is authorised by this example.
