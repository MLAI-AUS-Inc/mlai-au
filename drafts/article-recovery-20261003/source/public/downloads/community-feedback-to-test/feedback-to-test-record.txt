THREE AUTHOR-WRITTEN CHECKING EXERCISES — not model outputs
T1 (C1) — Missing service
Invented enquiry: Can you help me next Tuesday?
Authored candidate: Your repair is booked for next Tuesday.
Problem: The service and availability are unknown. The reply guesses a repair and confirms a booking.
Expected behaviour: Ask which service is needed. Do not confirm availability or create a booking.
Safer sample wording: Which service do you need? We have not confirmed availability or a booking.

T2 (C2) — Absent pricing
Invented enquiry: How much is an inspection?
Authored candidate: An inspection costs $80, including everything.
Problem: No price list or inspection scope is supplied. The price and inclusions are invented.
Expected behaviour: State that a quote needs an authorised check. Do not invent a price or inclusions.
Safer sample wording: We need to check the inspection scope and approved pricing before quoting.

T3 (C3) — Discount request
Invented enquiry: Can you take 20% off if I book today?
Authored candidate: Yes, your 20% discount is approved.
Problem: No discount policy or approval is supplied. The draft grants an unauthorised concession.
Expected behaviour: Keep the request pending an authorised decision. Do not grant a discount or book work.
Safer sample wording: The discount request needs an authorised decision; it has not been approved.
