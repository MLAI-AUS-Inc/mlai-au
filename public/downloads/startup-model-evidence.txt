FOUR FICTIONAL CASES
F1: A new local repair shop
Scenario: The owner wants one dependable neighbourhood location. More repairs require more technician hours and bench space.
Interpretation: A new local business under the stated plan, not a rapid-growth startup merely because it just opened.
Next question or action: Decide whether one location meets the owner’s goals. A later expansion plan would need separate evidence.

F2: An AI contractor proposing a shared quote-drafting product
Scenario: The current offer is bespoke workflow setup. A common template is proposed, but no shared product or customer trial exists.
Interpretation: A service business exploring a startup hypothesis. AI tooling and a reuse estimate do not prove repeatable delivery or demand.
Next question or action: Compare the actual setup differences before promising a product. The completed record below keeps that uncertainty visible.

F3: A software team testing one product for several businesses
Scenario: The team intends to serve many similar businesses. In this story two businesses agree to try a demonstration; neither has paid or returned for another use.
Interpretation: Startup intent and a possible repeatable offering, not demonstrated retention or a validated business model.
Next question or action: Record what the trial actually involves and whether the same offering solves a recurring need. Two agreements are not two retained customers.

F4: An established company trying an internal product
Scenario: A separate team is testing a new offer while the parent keeps running its established business.
Interpretation: The experiment may use startup-style discovery. That does not reclassify the whole parent as a newly founded company.
Next question or action: Name the unit being discussed. Check the actual applicant entity if considering a programme.

SETUP-HOUR COMPARISONS (PLANNING INPUTS, NOT MEASUREMENTS)
Same proposed task: set up a quote-drafting workflow; neither option automatically sends quotes.
Five assumed setups
Clients: 5; bespoke hours per client: 20; common hours: 40; shared hours per client: 4.
Bespoke: 5 x 20 = 100 hours.
Shared: 40 + 5 x 4 = 60 hours.
Difference (bespoke minus shared): 40 hours. Positive means fewer proposed shared hours.

Only one setup
Clients: 1; bespoke hours per client: 20; common hours: 40; shared hours per client: 4.
Bespoke: 1 x 20 = 20 hours.
Shared: 40 + 1 x 4 = 44 hours.
Difference (bespoke minus shared): -24 hours. Positive means fewer proposed shared hours.

More custom work
Clients: 5; bespoke hours per client: 20; common hours: 80; shared hours per client: 8.
Bespoke: 5 x 20 = 100 hours.
Shared: 80 + 5 x 8 = 120 hours.
Difference (bespoke minus shared): -20 hours. Positive means fewer proposed shared hours.

At the original rates, 40 + 4n < 20n only when n > 2.5; three whole setups. That arithmetic threshold does not establish that customers exist or the product is profitable.
Sales, support, maintenance, security and infrastructure are unestimated, not zero. Different integrations or approval rules may make tasks incomparable. No margin forecast, cash savings or validated business model is established.
